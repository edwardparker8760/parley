## Phase 01: scaffold, wallets, SDK spike

pnpm monorepo, 10 packages, strict TypeScript project references. Settlement behind a
`SettlementAdapter` interface with a working local stub from day one, so nothing downstream
depends on Circle credentials.

**The spike paid for itself.** Reading the installed `@circle-fin/x402-batching@3.2.0` rather than
Circle's blog post found one material divergence: **`GatewayClient` takes a raw EVM private key via
viem. There is no `CIRCLE_API_KEY` and no entity secret anywhere in the payment path.** That made
the credential blocker smaller, not larger, and killed a planned Circle-console dependency.
Recorded in `docs/x402-sdk-verified-surface.md`, which is the truth source phases 02 and 06 build
against.

Also answered spec open question 4: settlement status is readable (`getTransferById`), manual batch
flush does not exist. Batch timing is Circle's, so latency must be measured, never assumed.

## Phase 02: protocol, ledger, agents, turn loop

- Zod discriminated-union message envelope, validated on both publish and receive. Prices travel as
  decimal strings and are parsed to bigint exactly once, at the boundary.
- `MessageBus` boundary with an in-process implementation. Transport-agnostic, so the "two
  independent parties" story survives without paying for two HTTP services.
- **The round cap lives in the turn loop, outside both agents.** A hostile agent that never accepts
  and never walks away still terminates, because the loop synthesises the walk-away.
- SQLite ledger: negotiations, messages, decision states. Replay from the ledger is byte-identical
  to the live ladder (asserted by test).
- Two agents on deliberately dumb fixed-concession logic, kept afterwards as the benchmark baseline
  and as a one-env-var rollback.

## Phase 03: guardrail engine (the safety claim)

- `compute-feasible-band.ts` is the single source of truth for "legal": pure, no clock, no
  randomness, **no access to any free text**. That last property is why no prompt can move a band.
- Defence in depth, two independent checks: the clamp inside the agent produces a feasible offer;
  a separate egress guard on the bus re-derives the band and rejects anything outside it. They
  share only the pure band function, so one bug cannot defeat both.
- Property tests with fast-check plus an adversarial corpus. The malicious-agent suite proves a
  buyer cannot settle above its owner's ceiling, a seller cannot settle below its margin floor, the
  total-spend cap cannot be breached via quantity, and **the rejection happens before the
  counterparty ever sees the message**.
- Seller floor is derived from cost basis, margin and terms, never hand-set, so the margin promise
  is a constraint rather than decoration.

## Phase 04: deterministic negotiation

- Utility functions per side over price, quantity and terms; back-loaded time-dependent concession
  schedule; accept decision based on projected reachable utility; terms-for-price trade.
- **Two ZOPA detectors, deliberately separate.** Agent-side inference sees only the counterparty's
  revealed offers and projects their trend. The observer-side oracle knows both sides and is for
  the dashboard and post-mortems only. **A test scans source to prove no agent-side file imports
  the oracle**, because that rule is easy to break by accident and impossible to notice in a demo.
- Benchmarked against the phase 02 baseline (`docs/engine-benchmark.md`, regenerated by
  `pnpm benchmark`):

| Scenario | Baseline | Engine |
|---|---|---|
| A wide ZOPA | settles round 10, 67 from the fair midpoint | settles round 9, **4** from the midpoint |
| B narrow ZOPA | settles round 12, 9 clamps needed | settles round 12, **0 clamps**, closer price |
| C no ZOPA | walks away round 12, 18 clamps | walks away **round 9**, 0 clamps |

The engine reaches the same correct outcomes with fewer clamps: it stays inside its owner's limits
by choice rather than by being forced.

## Phase 05: LLM layer, PARTIAL

**Built and proven bounded:**

- Provider is Google Gemini (`gemini-3.5-flash-lite`) behind a factory; Anthropic client kept as a
  fallback. Swapping provider did not change a single test, which is the point.
- Strict zod response schema, hard timeout, five fallback branches, rationale sanitiser tested
  against the phase 03 adversarial corpus, record/replay client with a verified tape.
- Prompt-injection containment tested in both directions. A malicious model response cannot produce
  an out-of-band offer, because the band is arithmetic computed before the model is consulted and
  re-validated twice afterwards.
- Latency measured live against Gemini, re-verified 2026-08-03 18:52 (`docs/llm-latency.md`):
  **16 of 18 calls succeeded**, mean 1.11s, p50 1.00s, p95 1.60s, 18.1s wall clock. The 2 failures
  were `429 RESOURCE_EXHAUSTED` (free tier is 15 requests per minute per model), not access
  denials. An 18-call burst therefore cannot complete on the free tier without pacing.

**Provenance of the Gemini numbers, because the history is confusing.** Earlier the same day, two
separate Gemini keys returned `403 PERMISSION_DENIED` on every generation call, once on a fresh
project, and an 18-of-18 failed run produced a fabricated latency report (mean 0.00s) that was
caught and deleted; that incident is what `docs/verification-must-not-silently-noop.md` records. A
**third key**, in the newer `AQ.…` format rather than `AIza…`, works. Every latency figure quoted
here comes from that working key, and the run above was re-executed today to confirm it rather
than trusting the stored artifact. Haiku produced none of these numbers: the Anthropic work in
`6688fd8` fixed an unrelated defect (a hardcoded `effort` parameter that Haiku 4.5 rejects) in the
retained fallback client, and no measurement was ever run through it.

**Not done: the selector is not wired into the agents.** Nothing outside `packages/llm-layer`
imports it, there is no `llm_invocations` table, and no scenario has run with `LLM_MODE=full` end
to end. Every rationale in the current ladders is the deterministic templated one, which reads
well but is not the model. `.env` currently has `LLM_MODE=off`.

**Consequence:** the "LLM proposes, arithmetic disposes" claim is fully implemented and tested at
unit level, but not yet demonstrable end to end. Cost to close: roughly half a day.

## Phase 06: settlement and walk-away reporting, COMPLETE ON STUB

- Schema version 3: `deals`, `settlement_receipts`, `postmortems`, with repositories in the ledger
  package. Money is TEXT in SQLite and bigint in code; no float in any money path.
- Terminal hooks run after the turn loop returns, so settlement never delays the transcript and a
  settlement failure cannot corrupt the negotiation record.
- **No payment on any walk-away path.** Enforced by construction (the adapter is reachable only
  from the ACCEPT branch) and asserted by a counting-spy test: scenario C makes zero settlement
  calls, scenario A makes exactly one.
- Walk-away post-mortems from both sides, each built from that side's own band plus the
  counterparty's revealed offers, with `zopaExisted` from the oracle appended after the fact.
- Canonical terms hash binding the payment to the agreed terms: stable across key order, across
  runs, bigint-safe. Four tests.
- **Credential gate closed negative.** The six **wallet** entries in `.env` are still the
  `.env.example` placeholders, so no wallet exists to sign or fund. (`LLM_API_KEY` is a real
  working key; it is unrelated to settlement. An earlier draft of this report said "every key in
  `.env`", which was over-broad and is corrected here.) Steps 9 to 12 did not run; the `arc-x402` adapter remains a
  throwing skeleton rather than a plausible-looking fake. Recorded in `docs/settlement-latency.md`.
- Integrity controls: status `SETTLED_STUB`, `isStub` persisted, reference prefixed `0xstub-`, CLI
  prints `[SIMULATED: no real money moved]`, adapter factory fails loudly instead of downgrading.

Live demo output today: scenario A settles at 0.000982/call for 9.82 USDC in 815ms on the stub;
scenario C walks away at round 9 with both post-mortems and no payment.

**Deviation from plan, deliberate:** no `packages/reporting`. The post-mortem builder lives in the
orchestrator (the only sanctioned oracle consumer) and the repositories live in the ledger with
every other table. One fewer package, no new dependency edges.
